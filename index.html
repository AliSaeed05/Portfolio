Lunar-regolith rover dust cover

The dust-cover design for a rover’s collection subsystem, with robustness and manufacturability as the goals.

Role
Collections sub-team, Cardinal Space Mining; design lead for the dust cover
Status
In progress; in design stage
Dates
Current
Tools
SolidWorks CAD, ANSYS FEA, Milling, 3D printing
Case study: Lunar-regolith rover dust cover

Objective

Improve the robustness of the collection subsystem’s dust protection compared with the prior year’s design, while keeping the subsystem functional and manufacturable.

Why it matters

General context: lunar regolith is fine and abrasive, so a mechanism that moves material also has to keep it out of the parts that move.

My contribution

I lead the design of the dust cover as part of the Collections sub-team.

Design reasoning

The cover cannot be designed on its own. It sits where several requirements meet:

  • Protection and collection motion: the cover must not impede the parts it protects.
  • Clearance: gaps small enough to keep material out, large enough to avoid binding.
  • Attachment: how it mounts to the subsystem.
  • Fabrication: the team has to be able to build it.

Current stage

Design work is ongoing. A final design, prototype, and dust-protection test results do not exist yet.

Custom built loft-shelf

A custom-built loft-shelf designed and hand-built for personal use.

Role

Status
Ongoing; in manufacturing stage
Dates
October 2026 – present
Tools
SolidWorks CAD, ANSYS FEA, hand fabrication in wood, 3D printing
Case study: Custom built loft-shelf

Objective

Design, plan, and build a custom loft-shelf for personal use.

Design Considerations

The loft-shelf was designed to fit into my lofted bedframe and hold a variety of items. The design needed to be structurally sound, stable (no wobbling or shifting), while also being light-weight and easy to install or relocate if necessary.

Measurments of the bedframe were taken to fit the shelf brackets.

Wood was chosen as the material for the brackets as it is easy to manufacture, while being stiff and sturdy enough to bear the load of the shelf and its contents.

3D-printed PLA was chosen for the shelf itself as it is light-weight, reducing the total load on the brackets and bedframe. The 3D printing process also allowed for a more complex shape that would have been difficult to manufacture with wood.

The shelf was designed with a lip around the top surface to prevent items from sliding off, and sloted rails on the bottom surfaces to fit into the brackets without allowing for slipage or shifting. The shelf was also designed to be easily removable from the brackets if necessary.

An FEA Analysis was used to evaluate the structural integrity of the shelf and brackets, and to ensure that the design would be able to withstand the expected loads.

My contribution

This is a Solo Project. I measured the frame the shelf would be fitted to, and designed the shelf in SolidWorks. I also performed finite element analysis (FEA) in ANSYS to ensure the design would be structurally sound, and built the shelf by hand.

RL-Trained Robotic Hand

Trained a reinforcement learning model policy to control a robotic hand in Isaac Sim.

Role
Independent project
Status
Past project
Dates
May – July 2026
Tools
Python, Isaac Sim, modern reinforcement learning
Case study: RL-Trained Robotic Hand

Objective

Train a reinforcement learning model to control a robotic hand. Reference videos and object/hand trajectory data were collected from the HO-Cap (NeurIPS 2025) dataset. The dataset provided data on 21 key points on the robot and human hands, object position and rotation, object linear and angular velocity.

The policy was given the following observations:

  • The Robot's hand key points relative to the object
  • The Reference hand's key points relative to the object
  • The difference between the robot key points and the reference key points
  • The next-frame reference points relative to the object
  • Current joint positions
  • Contact forces acting on each fingertip

Tensorboard was also used to visualize the training process, including the reward function and the policy's performance over time.

Reward function

Some information on the reward function used to train the policy:

  • During initial training attempts, the agent seemed to have a difficult time learning to grip and pick up the object, even though it seemed to be following the reference path well. For this reason, a strong reward incentive was given for forming a strong, firm, and stable grip on the object.
  • Select points on the robots hand determined to be important for a firm grip (all 5 finger tips, palm, and base of the index and middle fingers) are tracked relative to their distance to the object. In the calculation for r_grasp, each of these points are weighted according to their important in forming a strong grip around the object.
  • The object's position and rotation are tracked relative to the reference object. The agent is rewarded for keeping the object’s pose as similar to the reference object as possible.
  • The amount and speed of movement of the robot’s hand is calculated and penalized. This discourages the agent from making too many unnecessary movements, and encourages a smooth and deliberate trajectory.

My contribution

This was a Solo Project. I trained the policy using reinforcement learning, and designed the reward function and action space.

Model rocket airbrake system

Proposed airbrake flight-mechanics system for a model rocket.

Role
Mechanical sub-team, Cyclone Rocketry; proposed airbrake design
Status
Completed
Dates
Oct 2024 – Dec 2025
Tools
SolidWorks, Ansys (FEA)
Case study: Model rocket airbrake system

Objective

Design an airbrake system that changes a rocket’s drag in flight, as part of designing and developing a model rocket with the Mechanical sub-team.

Design Considerations

The airbrakes add drag on demand to help a rocket reach a target apogee.

Previous year's designs, although robust, had a large footprint within the rocket body, and were too heavy. The airbrakes needed to be designed to fit within a smaller profile, while still being able to withstand the forces acting on them during flight.

The spiral cam design would allow for a more compact design without sacrificing control authority

As the fins deployed, the exponentially increasing drag would increase friction on the mechanism, so the design needed to be adjusted to have the fins riding on linear rails

The layout of the motors and how the mechanism fits into the rocket body had to be reconsidered, as the new design was very different from the previous year's.

My contribution

I proposed a airbrake concept using a spiral cam, allowing the mechanism to fit into a much smaller profile. I conducted finite element analysis (FEA) simulations in Ansys, and modeled the components in SolidWorks.

Substrate rubbing machine

A Substrate rubbing machine designed and built custom for the FMOD Research Lab.

Role
Project Manager, led design, construction, and testing
Status
Completed; deployed to lab
Dates
May - August 2024
Tools
Onshape, Arduino, hand fabrication, 3D printing
Case study: Substrate rubbing machine

Objective

Design, build, test, and deploy a substrate rubbing machine customized for the FMOD Research Lab's needs.

Design Considerations

In crystals research, substrate rubbing is used to induce anisotropy or align molecules. Usually this process is done by hand, which can be time consuming and inconsistent.

The FMOD research lab at LUMS needed a machine to automate the process of rubbing substrates. The machine needed to be able to accommodate different substrate sizes, and be able to apply a consistent amount of pressure and speed as a roller was pressed along the substrate. The machine also needed to be easy to operate, and be able to be cleaned and maintained easily.

The control buttons were designed to incorperate as much funtionality as possible, while still being simple to use. The automated rubbing routine would:

  • move the roller down to the correct height
  • move the substrate bed at a constant velocity under the roller
  • when the limit switch at the end of the substrate bed is triggered, the roller is lifted
  • the substrate bed is returned to the starting position

The controls also allow for manual positioning of the roller and substrate bed, and isolated control of the roller spin.

These control options were all designed considering the needs of the lab, and the need for a simple and easy to use interface.

My contribution

I led the design, construction, and testing of the substrate rubbing machine. With the assistance of the PhysLab team, I outlined the requirements and specifications of the machine, and then designed the machine in Onshape. I also designed a custom PCB for the button panel, 3D printed the control surfaces, and tested the machine before it was deployed to the lab.
